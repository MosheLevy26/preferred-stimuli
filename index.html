Preferred Stimuli

Literal Abstract

These are feature visualizations from ConvNeXt Medium, an image model with 7,057,377 parameters. Each one starts as random noise. The pixels get adjusted until one neuron fires as hard as it can. The result shows what that neuron responds to. The model learned all of this on its own while it was trained to sort photos into 1,000 categories.

Goodfire’s microscope has one of these for each of the model’s 8,832 neurons. I picked 15. The first 8 are the most literal ones, where you can see the thing if you squint. The other 7 are the most abstract ones that still look clean. Click any of them to see the real photos that make its neuron fire the most.

Literal

These are from late in the model, where a lot of neurons respond to whole objects. All 8 see the whole image. 7 of them are from the last stage.

Abstract

These are from early in the model, where neurons mostly respond to color and pattern. 6 of the 7 are from stage 1, where each neuron sees a patch 128 pixels wide. The other one is from stage 2.

The images and photos are from Goodfire’s microscope for ConvNeXt Medium, which is part of Neural Networks Are Already Clean by Nick Cammarata. The photos come from the ImageNet validation set (50,000 images).

Goodfire made the images with an FFT image parameterization, blur annealing and augmentation, using the model’s epoch 90 checkpoint (72.0% top-1).
